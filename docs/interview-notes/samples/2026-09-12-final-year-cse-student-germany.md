# Interview (SAMPLE): final-year CSE student applying to Germany, 2026-09-12

| | |
|---|---|
| **Date** | 2026-09-12 |
| **Role** | Final-year B.Tech CSE student (tier-2 college) |
| **Where they are, where they want to work** | Hyderabad → Germany (Berlin or Munich) |
| **How we met** | In person, college canteen; friend of a classmate |
| **Who spoke to them** | Rohan |
| **Length** | 35 minutes |
| **Consent to quote** | Yes, with first name (Sai Teja, fictional) |

## What they said

- Has applied to about 60 roles since July, mostly on LinkedIn and StepStone. Two replies, both rejections.
- One application takes him "almost an hour" because he rewrites the resume for every job, then gives up and sends the same one. _(paraphrase)_
- Finds out about visa sponsorship only after the first call, or never. "Half the JDs are in German, I put them in Google Translate."
- Has paid ₹3,500 for a resume review on Instagram that "just changed the fonts".
- Would pay ₹1,000–2,000 a month if he could see who checked his application and why a job was picked for him.

## One line in their own words

> "I don't need more jobs, I need to know which ten I should actually send." _(sample)_

## What surprised us

- He cared more about the *reason* a job was suggested than about the number of jobs.
- Language requirements (B1 German) were a bigger filter than visa sponsorship for him.

## Did they agree with our idea?

**Yes.** Liked the human review step most; doubtful anyone would trust a fully automatic bot.

## What we changed because of this conversation

- Added "language required" as a signal in matching and showed match reasons on every job card.
